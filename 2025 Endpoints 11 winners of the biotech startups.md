 superintelligent AI build a pipeline at the frontier of science? Can developers finally break down the barriers to treating solid tumors with in vivo CAR-T? Can the inherent promise of oncolytics be harnessed more effectively to destroy cancer cells?

These are just a few of the questions asked by this year’s Endpoints 11 — our annual list of biotech’s most promising young startups.

All are backed by big names in science and investing. Some of our honorees are traveling down pathways that have [[confounded]] scientists for decades. Others are determined to break completely new ground with fresh insights. All see an urgent need to demonstrate promise with human data — discarding achingly slow trial strategies in favor of a new, global approach that puts top operators around the world to work for some very small biotechs.

Everyone knows that biotech faces some enormous challenges. But we’re here to celebrate some of the best-known players in the biotech business who are still willing to swing for the fences. Because we also know that the science has never been better, with fresh opportunities to transform the way diseases are treated, and sometimes cured.

Of course, not all of them are going to make it big — as a review of our past Endpoints 11 lists shows. The odds of failure, or just running out of time and money, dictate a high mortality rate. What we are sure of is that the teams here are all-in on doing something important.

On a personal note, I made my return to the Endpoints 11 event in Boston after advanced Merkel cell carcinoma left me in the hospital last summer. I’m still here because, 15 years ago, teams of developers believed that immunotherapy could transform treatment for huge numbers of cancer patients.

Because of their work, and because they were right, I’m in remission. Staying alive may well require that one of this year’s biotech players comes through with another wave of innovation. The stakes are high — and I’m not just talking about money here.

The Endpoints 11 represent the best and the brightest in biotech. They deserve careful attention. And we’ll be following every step.
